# 2-channel phantom-powered mic preamp — KiCad sample

Owner: Hai (GitHub `haiha-ux`). Test/portfolio project, not client work.

KiCad 10.0.6 project: schematic + 2-layer 100 × 78 mm PCB, autorouted, with ERC/DRC
reports, fab outputs and renders. Every file is generated by the scripts in `tools/`,
so the whole design can be rebuilt from scratch.

## What is on the board

Per channel (×2):

| Block | Parts | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Input | Neutrik NC3FAH1 female XLR, horizontal (pin 1 GND, 2 hot, 3 cold) | — |
| Phantom feed | 2 × 6.81 kΩ 1 % **2010 (0.75 W)** to a common node, 3-pin jumper ON (1-2 → +48 V) / OFF (2-3 → 47 kΩ to GND) | INA163 datasheet Fig. 5 (R1, R2, R3) |
| DC block | 2 × 47 µF 63 V **bi-polar** electrolytic | Fig. 5 note (1): non-polar required when phantom can be switched off |
| Input protection | BAV99 dual diode per leg, clamping to ±15 V, then 47 Ω in series to the IC | Fig. 5 text ("four diodes to the supplies"); 47 Ω taken from Fig. 2 |
| Bias return | 2 × 2.2 kΩ to GND | Fig. 5 (R4, R5) |
| Preamp | TI **INA163UA** (SOIC-14), G = 1 + 6000/RG, RG = 60.4 Ω → G = 100.3 (40.0 dB) | gain equation and table, datasheet p. 6 |
| Decoupling | 100 nF X7R on each rail at the IC | Fig. 1 ("0.1 µF near the device pins") |

Shared: ±15 V header J3 with 10 µF on each rail and SMAJ16A TVS rail clamps (D21/D22);
+48 V header J4 with 47 µF/100 V + 100 nF; line-out header J5 (OUT1/GND/OUT2/GND); 4 × M3 holes.

Datasheet used: TI SBOS177D (INA163), checked page by page. Pin-out from p. 2, gain from
p. 6, application circuit from Fig. 5 on p. 8.

## Check results (final board)

- **ERC: 0 errors, 0 warnings.**
- **DRC: 0 errors, 0 unconnected, 0 schematic-parity issues.** There are 4 warnings, all
  waived (see below).
- **Netlist check:** the `kicad-cli` netlist is compared pin-by-pin with the intended net
  table (`tools/check_netlist.py`). It matches 100 %. The check has a built-in sabotage
  case: moving one pin to another net must make it fail.
- **Was the 48 V clearance rule actually applied?** On a copy of the board the rule was
  raised from 0.6 mm to 5 mm, and DRC reported 289 "P48 creepage" violations. So the rule
  really matches the 48 V nets; it is not passing trivially.
- **Drill file:** the hits in the Excellon files were counted (182 PTH + 8 NPTH) and match
  the pads and vias on the board.

### Waived DRC items

| Item | Count | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `silk_edge_clearance`, J1/J2 | 4 | The NC3FAH1 front flange sits in front of the panel line, which the footprint marks on Dwgs.User at x = 12.7 mm. The board edge is placed on that panel line, so the flange outline overhangs the board by design. |

### Design-rule decisions (they change what "0 errors" means)

- **Board minimum track width is 0.15 mm** (KiCad default is 0.2 mm). Freerouting narrows
  0.25 mm signal tracks to 0.19 mm where they enter the SOIC-14 pads. JLCPCB/PCBWay handle
  0.1 mm.
- **±15 V tracks are ≥ 0.4 mm.** Inside the U1/U2 and decoupling-cap courtyards they may
  narrow to 0.3 mm. Those are local stubs carrying a few mA. This is the
  `Power neck-down` rule in the `.kicad_dru` file.
- **All nets that can carry 48 V have ≥ 0.6 mm clearance.** That is +48V, PH1/2, XLRx_HOT,
  XLRx_COLD and P48OFF1/2. The value is IPC-2221B table 6-1, column B2 (external, uncoated,
  31–150 V). Solder mask was not counted as a coating, which is the conservative choice.
- **INA163 Ref pin (pin 10) is tied solidly to the GND pour, with no thermal relief.**
  The datasheet (p. 6) says a few ohms in series with Ref degrades CMRR.
- **GND pads of J3/J4/J5 are also solid.** With thermal relief, signal tracks blocked the
  spokes and DRC reported `starved_thermal`. `tools/build_pcb.py::fix_starved` applies the
  same treatment to any GND pad that DRC still reports as starved; none on the final board.
- **GND is poured on both layers, with 117 stitching vias** on a 6 mm grid, kept ≥ 1 mm
  from other nets.

## Deviations from the brief / datasheet — please review

1. **Phantom "switch" is a 3-pin jumper header (Molex 22-28-4030, 500 V) plus a shunt,
   not a slide switch.** Every small PCB slide switch checked has a DC rating below the
   48 V ±4 V of IEC 61938:
   - C&K OS102011: 12 VDC
   - Würth 450301014042: 12 VDC
   - E-Switch EG1271: 30 V
   - E-Switch EG1224: 15 VDC
   - C&K 7101 toggle: 28 VDC

   The header doubles as the connection point for a panel-mount switch. Its OFF position
   discharges the node through 47 kΩ, the same as R3 in datasheet Fig. 5.
2. **Gain is fixed at 40 dB** (the brief said "gain set by one resistor"). Change R8/R18
   using the RG table on p. 6.
3. **The optional DC servo of Fig. 5 (OPA134) is not fitted, so the output is DC-coupled.**
   Estimated DC offset at G = 100, from datasheet values:
   - typical: about 7 mV from Vos plus 22 mV from Ios × 2.2 kΩ;
   - worst case: about 30 mV plus 220 mV.

   **The next stage should be AC-coupled.**
4. **The C1/C2 type (Nichicon UVP1J470MPD) is marked "Not For New Designs" on DigiKey.**
   Cornell Dubilier 476BPS063M (active) is listed as the alternative. Neither is stocked at
   LCSC, so the board is not fully JLC-assemblable as is.
5. **BAV99 is used instead of 1N4148**, with the same function in a smaller package
   (Nexperia BAV99: 100 V, 4 A surge per the LCSC listing).

## Things I am not sure about

- **XLR footprint.** I used the stock KiCad `Jack_XLR_Neutrik_NC3FAH1_Horizontal`. The
  Neutrik/LCSC drawing download was blocked, so I did **not** check its hole sizes and
  peg positions against the manufacturer drawing myself. There is also no 3D model for it
  in the KiCad library, so the renders show only the footprint outline.
- **Shell and pin 1.** By its description, the NC3FAH1 ties the mating shell to pin 1 and
  to the front panel. Pin 1 goes straight to board GND at the connector. In a metal box
  this bonds chassis to signal ground at both XLRs. That is in the spirit of AES48
  (pin 1 to chassis at the entry), but the ground return of the ±15 V supply then needs
  care to avoid a loop. Not analysed.
- **P48 hot-plug.** A shorted XLR line dumps the 48 V charge of C1/C2 through the BAV99
  diodes into the rails. I added the SMAJ16A rail clamps for that, but **there is no surge
  simulation or test**. The INA163 datasheet does not cover this case. From memory (not
  re-read for this job), THAT Corp's "The 48 Volt Phantom Menace" (AES, 2009) shows
  stronger fixes: higher-current steering diodes and small series resistors. Those were
  not added because they cost noise.
- **Creepage value.** I chose the conservative 0.6 mm. If solder mask is counted as a
  coating (IPC-2221B A6/B4), about 0.13 mm would be enough.
- **Footprint height for C1/C2.** The bi-polar caps use `C_Radial_D10.0mm_H20.0mm_P5.00mm`;
  the real part is 17.5 mm tall. The 2D land pattern is the same; only the 3D height is off.
- **No SPICE simulation.** Gain, noise and offset come from the datasheet equations only.
- **Routing quality.** The board is autorouted by Freerouting and not hand-tuned. The hot
  and cold input traces of a channel are not length- or path-matched. At audio
  frequencies this matters little electrically, but a human layout would keep each pair
  together.

## Tool chain and how to rebuild

- KiCad 10.0.6. The schematic is written only through the Konnect MCP server (`tools/kcall.py`
  drives it over stdio); the `.kicad_sch` file is never edited as text.
- The PCB is built with KiCad's own `pcbnew` Python API.
- Routing: Freerouting 2.4.1 on Temurin JRE 25. The native `freerouting-cli` 2.5.0 crashed
  with a GraalVM serialization error.
- Parts and MPNs come from the JLCPCB/LCSC parts database (813k parts, local copy), plus
  DigiKey lookups for the bi-polar caps and the switch ratings.

To rebuild:

```bash
python tools/build_schematic.py      # move the old .kicad_sch out of the way first
python tools/check_netlist.py
"C:/Program Files/KiCad/10.0/bin/python.exe" tools/build_pcb.py all   # with KiCad closed
bash tools/export_outputs.sh
```
